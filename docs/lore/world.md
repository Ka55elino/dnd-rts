# World

Status: `draft`

## Name

The world has one common name: **Oikouménē**, meaning the inhabited or known world.

### One name, no split

There is no strict distinction between an insider name and a name used on maps. Different peoples may have their own names for the world, shaped by their languages, cultures, myths, and understanding of its origins, but these variations are not a priority for the lore.

### Usage

For now, Oikouménē serves as the universal name used in worldbuilding documents, maps, and other references. Culture-specific names can be introduced later when they add meaningful depth to a faction's identity or worldview.

## The essence in two sentences

Oikouménē is a world where different peoples struggle over faith, truth, and the right way to live.

### Many faiths, one reality

Each civilization has its own religions, myths, and beliefs about the nature of existence, shaping its laws, traditions, and ambitions. Though these faiths appear fundamentally different, they are interpretations of the same underlying reality: a world shaped by primordial energies, not divine will.

### Conflict of convictions

Yet shared origins do not bring shared understanding. Peoples clash over what they believe to be true, and their convictions can unite civilizations, justify conquest, or drive them to war.

### No absolute good or evil

No race is inherently good or evil, and no faith holds a universal monopoly on morality.

## Foundation of the universe

The world arose from **primordial energy**, the fundamental component of the universe from which all that exists began. Its concentrations became the centers around which the world took shape.

Magic originates from the same primordial forces (see [`magic.md`](magic.md)). There are no gods in the traditional sense: the world was formed by these forces, not created by divine will.

## What makes the world special

### 1. Magic is a natural force, not a divine gift

Magic originates from primordial energy particles that helped shape the world and its inhabitants. It is part of the world's natural structure rather than a power granted by gods. Its manifestations can be studied, harnessed and used, even though its ultimate origin remains unknown.

### 2. Religions describe the same reality through different myths

No religion has a confirmed divine source or exclusive access to the truth. Each people has developed its own creation myths, sacred figures and explanations of the world's origins. These beliefs may contradict one another, even when they reflect different interpretations of the same underlying phenomena.

### 3. The world's true origins are unknown

There is no universally accepted creation story, and no known creator deity behind existence. The world emerged through the influence of primordial energies, but how these energies came to exist remains a mystery. Every civilization preserves its own account of the beginning, with none proven to be the definitive explanation.

### 4. Conflict arises from incompatible worldviews, not absolute good and evil

The peoples of Oikouménē have different ideas about order, freedom, nature, authority and the sacred. Their religions and traditions shape how they understand the world and justify their actions. Conflicts emerge when these beliefs and interests collide, rather than from a universal division between inherently good and inherently evil peoples.
