# Dwarves

Status: `draft`

## Concept (from the mechanics)

The people under the mountains: sturdy armor, gunpowder and machines, runes and golems, underground tunnels. Slow but inexorable.

## Who they are

- **What they call themselves and what others call them:** **Dragori**.
- **Where they came from, how old they are as a people:** one of the first civilizations of Oikouménē; took shape during the Age of Unification (about 500 years, see [`../history.md`](../history.md)).
- **Where they live** (see [`../geography.md`](../geography.md)): the capital Monsarx, in the heart of the highest massif of the northern mountains, a center of engineering, craft and runic art, with airship docks; Järngród, an underground city in the rock not far from Kordkayer, one of the main industrial centers, the first to find the Empire and start trading with it; Skopeskala, a young city on the eastern ridge, at the edge of the settled lands, still under construction; the cities are linked by underground tunnels. There are no other dwarven settlements.
- **How society works** (power, estates, faith): a union of ancient houses under the King under the Mountain and the Council of Houses; see [Society](#society).

- **Their "gray" side** (mixed tone): see [Gray side](#gray-side) below.

## Society

The dwarves are united not in a single kingdom with the absolute power of a monarch, but in a union of ancient houses. Each house keeps a particular legacy: metallurgy, stonecutting, construction, mechanisms, the craft of war or the study of the depths.

A house is at once a large family, a professional organization, a political faction and the owner of particular workshops, mines and parts of the city.

- **The royal house** is responsible for the unity of the dwarves, foreign policy and defense.
- **Houses of masters** control production, training and the quality of goods.
- **Houses of stone** are responsible for mines, tunnels, foundations and fortifications.
- **War houses** organize the heavy infantry, the artillery and the defense of the underground roads.
- **Lesser houses** handle trade, farming in the mountain valleys, scouting and the upkeep of the cities.

Membership in a house is usually inherited, but one's position within it also depends on personal mastery. A dwarf can bring glory to their house with an outstanding invention, but talent alone is not enough to gain political power right away.

### Who rules

The supreme ruler is the **King under the Mountain**. His authority is recognized by all the great houses, but he cannot freely dispose of their mines, workshops and hereditary holdings.

The main decisions are made by the **Council of Houses**.

| Who | Responsible for |
|---|---|
| The King | war, diplomacy, the unity of the kingdom |
| The Council of Houses | laws, taxes, the distribution of resources |
| Senior masters | production standards, training, technical decisions |
| Keepers of stone | mines, tunnels, safety and the exploration of the depths |
| War houses | the army, fortresses, guarding the underground roads |

## Gunpowder

Gunpowder came from mining. The dwarves were looking for ways to break especially hard rock that could not be mined efficiently with ordinary tools. Experiments with minerals and combustible mixtures led to the discovery of an explosive compound.

At first its use was dangerous and limited. Only after several generations of testing did stable technologies for its production and safe storage appear.

This created three lines of development:

1. **Mining:** breaking hard rock and driving new workings.
2. **Defense:** heavy cannons to protect gates and mountain passes.
3. **Warfare:** small squads of gunners able to break fortifications and stop the advance of large armies.

## Tunnels

The dwarves dug the tunnels themselves over many generations. They hold mines, storehouses, ventilation shafts, drainage channels and routes for hauling cargo.

These tunnels are well explored, marked on maps and controlled by the houses of stone.

## Gray side

The Dwarves value craftsmanship, endurance, loyalty, and the preservation of what their ancestors built. However, their commitment to their own people often outweighs any sense of responsibility toward the wider world. They may refuse to aid neighboring kingdoms unless their own interests are secured, even when entire settlements are threatened. Their mines and forges bring prosperity, but their pursuit of resources can scar the mountains and displace communities living nearby.

## Faith

**The Tradition of the First Giants**: the mountains are the legacy of the First Giants; no single church — elders, rune masters and keepers of clan traditions; oaths and ancestral memory are sacred. Full description: [`../religions.md`](../religions.md#dwarves--the-tradition-of-the-first-giants).

## Relations

At the start of the game the peoples meet for the first time (first contact, see [`../history.md`](../history.md)): there are no established relations yet. Attitudes toward other peoples follow from the faith (see "Faith" above) and arise during the game.

## Notes

Machines (airships, golems) are the dwarves' own inventions, like gunpowder.

What lies in the depths beyond the explored tunnels is unknown.

The dwarves of Järngród and the Empire have only just found each other and started trading; there are no embassies yet.

### Worker

The main labor force of the Dragori. Workers mine stone and ore, raise fortifications, repair buildings and maintain engineering structures. Well suited to work in mines and underground tunnels.

**Appearance:** a stocky build, work clothes, a leather apron, protective goggles, a tool belt. A pickaxe, a hammer or a small engineering device. Unlike warriors, they wear almost no metal armor.

### Axe Dwarf

The main combat foot soldier of the Dragori. Armed with a heavy axe, well protected and able to endure long clashes. Effective against ordinary infantry and light units, but not fast.

**Appearance:** massive iron or steel armor, the characteristic beard, a wide belt and a heavy two-handed axe that emphasizes the offensive specialization.

### Gunner

A specialized shooter using the Dragori's own gunpowder weapons. A shot deals high damage and is effective against armored targets. But the weapon takes time to reload, so gunners are vulnerable to a sudden attack.

**Appearance:** a short heavy gun or hand cannon, metal parts, gunpowder accessories, protective gloves and armor. The weapon must look like an early fantasy firearm, not a modern rifle.

### Airship

A flying engineering construction built by Dragori masters. Used for scouting, moving across the battlefield and supporting ground units from the air. Can carry a small crew and weapons.

**Appearance:** a large gas envelope, a metal gondola, a wooden hull, screws or propellers, ropes and bronze mechanisms. It is important to keep a medieval fantasy aesthetic, without excessive steampunk. Armament: a light swivel cannon.

### Mortar

A short-barreled artillery piece for high-angle fire with heavy shells. Effective against clusters of infantry, fortifications and buildings. Takes time to prepare a shot and cannot defend itself effectively in close combat.

**Appearance:** a massive short barrel of bronze or iron, a sturdy wooden or metal platform, an aiming mechanism and ammunition. The crew consists of several dwarves.

### Bear Rider

Rare warriors who use specially trained large bears. This cavalry combines the strength of rider and beast, can break through enemy formations and fights heavy infantry effectively. Bears lack the speed of horses but far surpass them in physical strength.

**Appearance:** a large brown or dark bear, a massive leather harness with metal elements, armored sections on the body. The rider wears heavy armor and is armed with an axe or a hammer. It must look like a tradition of a mountain people, not like ordinary cavalry with the horse swapped out.

### Golem

A construction made by Dragori masters and brought to life by magic and ancient runes. Golems are used to protect settlements, storm fortifications and fight the most dangerous enemies. They are very durable and physically strong, but move slowly and are expensive.

**Appearance:** a massive humanoid construction of stone and metal plates joined by mechanisms and runic inserts. Inside the body is an energy core that gives off a glow. It should not be a fully metal robot: it must look like a combination of ancient magic and dwarven engineering.
