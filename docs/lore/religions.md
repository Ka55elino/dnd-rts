# Religions

Status: `draft`

Religion is the main difference and the main source of conflict between the peoples of Oikouménē. There are no gods in the traditional sense (see [`magic.md`](magic.md)): every faith below is a people's own explanation of a world shaped by primordial energies, and none of them is confirmed as the absolute truth.

| People | Faith | Core image | Inspiration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human Empire | [The Faith of the One Light](#human-empire--the-faith-of-the-one-light) | the primordial Light that gave form to existence; one moral order | Catholic Christianity |
| Dark Elves | [The Cult of the Great Spider](#dark-elves--the-cult-of-the-great-spider) | the Great Weave spun by the Great Spider | D&D dark elf mythology, spider cults |
| Dwarves | [The Tradition of the First Giants](#dwarves--the-tradition-of-the-first-giants) | the mountains as the remains and legacy of the First Giants | Norse mythology, ancestor cult |
| Alkars | [The Faith of the Eternal Cycle](#alkars--the-faith-of-the-eternal-cycle) | life and death as parts of one process | the D&D Death domain, natural cycles |
| High Elves | [The Tradition of the Living World](#high-elves--the-tradition-of-the-living-world) | the balance of natural forces | Slavic mythology, animism |

## Human Empire — the Faith of the One Light

**Inspiration:** Catholic Christianity — a church hierarchy, sacred texts, monastic orders, cathedrals, paladins and the idea of a single moral order.

### Core beliefs

- The world arose from the primordial Light, which gave form to a previously formless existence.
- Life is part of the established world order, and reason allows living beings to tell right from wrong.
- Magic is seen as a manifestation of forces laid into the fabric of creation, but its use must obey moral principles.
- Death ends earthly life, after which a person returns to the established world order. Specific ideas about the afterlife may differ between church movements.
- A person is responsible for their deeds and must maintain order in the family, society and the state.

### Organization

The religion has a centralized church hierarchy — the Holy Order of the paladins (see [`factions/empire.md`](factions/empire.md#spiritual-power--the-holy-order)). At its head stands the highest spiritual dignitary; below are regional hierarchs, priests and monastic communities.

The church is engaged in education, the keeping of written knowledge, charity and spiritual guidance. Knightly orders protect holy sites, pilgrims and church lands.

### Attitude toward other peoples

The imperial church considers its teaching the most complete and correct understanding of creation. Other religions are seen as delusions that arose from incomplete knowledge.

However, this does not necessarily mean a demand to convert everyone by force. Some church movements may support missionary work and the conversion of unbelievers; others prefer peaceful coexistence.

## Dark Elves — the Cult of the Great Spider

**Inspiration:** D&D dark elf mythology, spider cults and myths of the world's creation through weaving.

Unlike the religion of humans, which ties the world to order and Light, the dark elves see the world as a great Weave spun by the Great Spider.

### Core beliefs

- Before the world appeared there was a formless void. The Great Spider spun the first Weave from it, giving rise to all that exists.
- Each thread of the Weave stands for a life, a fate, a bloodline, power or a bond between beings.
- Everything living is part of the Weave. No being exists fully independently of the others.
- Death is the breaking of a thread, but not necessarily the disappearance of its meaning. The consequences of deeds continue to affect the Weave.
- Strength, cunning and the ability to control other threads are considered signs of superiority.
- Weakness and carelessness are seen as the risk of being consumed by another's will.

### The Great Spider

The Great Spider is not just a patron of spiders. It is the creator of the world and the highest embodiment of power, mystery and the interweaving of fates.

Its depictions vary: a huge spider, a figure with spider limbs, or a silhouette hidden behind countless threads. No one knows what it should really look like.

Its existence as a real deity is not confirmed. The dark elves believe in it, and its image arose from their mythology and their observations of nature.

### The Spider Queen

The Spider Queen is the high priestess of the Great Spider and the head of the cult. She is not a goddess but a living being, endowed with far greater power than others. Her transformation into a giant spider is a spell based on primordial energy, not a gift of the Great Spider (see [`magic.md`](magic.md)). She can fight, she can be wounded and defeated (Dark Elf hero, see [`factions/dark-elves.md`](factions/dark-elves.md)).

### Organization

The cult is closely tied to the matriarchal system of the dark elves. Priestesses hold considerable spiritual and political influence, and religious rites can at the same time serve as tools for governing society.

The spider, the web and weaving are the main symbols of the religion. Temples resemble complex networks of stone arches, bridges, suspended walkways and thin thread-like ornaments.

### Attitude toward other peoples

The dark elves consider other religions mistaken attempts to understand the Weave. Foreign gods and myths may be seen as false names given to phenomena that other peoples are unable to comprehend.

## Dwarves — the Tradition of the First Giants

**Inspiration:** Norse mythology, primordial giants, the world tree, runic symbolism and the cult of ancestors.

The dwarves believe that the mountains under which they built their kingdoms are the legacy of the First Giants — ancient beings that existed before the present peoples appeared.

### Core beliefs

- At the beginning of the world there were the First Giants, whose bodies and deeds formed the earthly foundation of the world.
- The mountains arose from their bones, rocks and petrified remains; the deep caves from the voids left by their disappearance.
- Underground rivers, metals and gems are considered traces of ancient forces locked in the earth.
- The dwarves see themselves as keepers of the mountain legacy. Their task is not simply to extract resources, but to preserve and strengthen what was created before them.
- Oaths, ancestral memory and faithfulness to one's word have special spiritual significance. A broken oath destroys not only a relationship, but also the order that binds the generations.
- Death returns the body to the earth, while deeds and the name live on in the memory of the clan.

### The First Giants

The Giants are not gods. In dwarven mythology they are the most ancient beings, who shaped the world with their actions and then disappeared.

Some legends say they died, others that they fell asleep deep underground, and others still that they turned into the very foundation of the world.

The dwarves do not know which version is true. But each of them explains why the mountains are considered not just a place to live, but the legacy of an ancient era.

### Organization

The religion has no single church. Its keepers are elders, rune masters and the keepers of clan traditions.

Sacred places are ancient halls, deep mines, stone circles, forges and the tombs of ancestors. Runes are used not only for writing, but also to record oaths, genealogies and the memory of great deeds.

### Attitude toward other peoples

The dwarves consider their traditions true and other peoples' creation stories mistaken. At the same time, their faith does not require them to convert other peoples to their religion.

Desecrating tombs, destroying ancient halls or seizing ancestral mountains may be seen as grave sacrilege.

## Alkars — the Faith of the Eternal Cycle

**Inspiration:** the D&D Death domain, natural cycles, ancient burial traditions and the philosophy of balance.

The most philosophical of the five faiths. It is not built around worshipping death as destruction. Its central idea is that life and death are not opposing forces but successive parts of one process.

### Core beliefs

- Everything that exists passes through cycles of emergence, change and disappearance.
- Life cannot continue forever in an unchanging form. Death makes room for new existence.
- Death in itself is neither good nor evil. It is natural, although the circumstances of a death can be unjust or tragic.
- Breaking the natural cycle is considered dangerous. For example, artificially keeping the dead in the world of the living can be seen as a distortion of the order.
- Life is sacred not because it must last forever, but because every living being is part of a continuous process.
- The memory of the dead is important, but holding on to them at any cost is considered wrong.

### Death and the afterlife

The Alkars do not know for certain what happens after death.

Their faith teaches that an individual life ends and its components return to the common circle of existence. What exactly remains after death — a soul, memory, energy or only the consequences of deeds — is a matter of different interpretations.

This fits Oikouménē as a whole: religion offers an explanation, but has no confirmed knowledge of the afterlife.

### Organization

Instead of a single centralized church, the Alkars have communities of keepers of the cycle. They perform burial rites, tend burial places, help the dying and see that traditions are kept.

Sacred places can be found in ancient groves, stone sanctuaries and at springs. The symbols are a closed circle, a withering leaf next to a new sprout, and a lantern marking the transition between states.

Priests may use magic connected with death, but this does not mean they have to be necromancers. On the contrary, some of them may consider raising the dead unnatural.

### Breaking the cycle for balance

The faith holds a deliberate contradiction. Death is an important part of the cycle, and yet the cycle may be broken when this is needed to maintain balance. This is why the Alkars raise the fallen (the dark acolytes' curse, the wraiths), why the Wraith King, himself already one of the dead, guards the Alkarian Necropolis, and why the necropolis exists at all. Where exactly the line lies between a justified break and a distortion of the order is a matter of constant dispute — this is the "gray" side of the faith.

### Attitude toward other peoples

The Alkars consider other religions delusions, but their own teaching does not necessarily require fighting unbelievers. They can cooperate with members of other cultures, especially in matters of healing, burial and preserving knowledge.

Conflicts arise when other peoples seek to defeat death at any cost, desecrate burials or use the dead as a tool.

## High Elves — the Tradition of the Living World

**Inspiration:** Slavic mythology, animism, the veneration of natural phenomena, sacred groves, springs, lakes and ancient rites.

The high elves do not worship a single deity or a pantheon. Their religion comes from the conviction that nature has its own order, and the living world cannot be separated from the forces that make it exist.

Unlike humans, who see a single moral order in creation, the elves perceive it as an interconnection of natural forces that succeed one another and sustain the existence of the world.

### Core beliefs

- Forests, rivers, lakes, the wind and celestial phenomena have spiritual meaning.
- Natural forces are not necessarily persons with human characters. They can be revered without imagining them as separate gods.
- The world is sustained by a balance between growth, withering, movement, rest and renewal.
- Magic is a natural part of the world, like the flow of water or the movement of the wind.
- A human, an elf or any other thinking people does not stand outside nature and has no unconditional right to dispose of it.
- Sacred places must be preserved, and interfering with the natural order requires caution and an understanding of the consequences.

### Spirits and natural forces

Folk legends may contain images of forest spirits, keepers of the waters, beings connected with the winds and other manifestations of natural forces.

Whether these beings really exist as separate thinking entities is deliberately left open. They may be part of elven folklore, symbolic images or interpretations of real magical phenomena. This keeps freedom for developing the world further without turning every natural force into a separate god.

### Organization

The high elves have no centralized church. The spiritual role is played by the keepers of sacred groves, sages, elders and communities responsible for particular natural places.

Rites are held according to the change of seasons, the phases of the moon, the flooding of rivers and other natural events.

The architecture of sanctuaries fits organically into the surroundings: open altars, stone circles, bridges over water and structures around ancient trees.

### Attitude toward other peoples

The elves may consider the religions of other peoples false, but do not necessarily see the outsiders themselves as enemies. The main criterion for judging a foreign civilization is its attitude toward the living world.

A people that cuts down sacred forests or destroys ancient natural places for resources may be seen as a threat. However, the elves are able to form alliances with those who respect the natural order, even if they profess a completely different religion.
